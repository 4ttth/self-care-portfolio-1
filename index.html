How I take care of myself

My self-care practices, split into four parts of me.

Physical

Sleep. I make sure I get enough sleep, around seven to eight hours on school nights, and I take naps when my body needs it.

Eating well. I eat real meals and not just snacks. I love food, so eating something good is also how I recharge.

Emotional

K-dramas and movies as a reward. I finish one task first, then I watch an episode. That way it's a break and not a way to run from my work.

Writing haikus. When I can't explain how I feel, I write it in seventeen syllables. Here's one from a late night of training:

Late night, screen still on,

one more challenge, then I sleep.

Tomorrow, the rest.

F.O.

Social

Talking with my family. I talk with my family at dinner or whenever we're all home. Telling them about my day, the good and the stressful parts, makes it feel lighter.

Even a short talk reminds me I'm not carrying everything by myself.

Rice still warm, we talk

about school, fares, and small things.

The day gets lighter.

Spiritual

Taking pictures. Photography makes me slow down and notice small things, like the sky on the way home or light hitting the road. It helps me feel calm and thankful for what I have.

Quiet time to reflect. I take a few minutes to look back on my day, sometimes through a haiku, and remember why I'm working this hard.

Jeepney in the rain,

I count coins for the fare home.

p. 4

Why this plan helps me

This plan helps my wellbeing because it fits my real life. My biggest stressors are school, my cybersecurity trainings, money, and the commute, and most of them won't go away anytime soon. What I can change is how I handle them. Breaking my work into small tasks helps me fight procrastination, because a small task is easier to start even when I'm tired. Doing one task at a time also keeps my head from feeling full. Setting fixed training days means my competitions and my schoolwork don't fight for the same night. Writing down my expenses gives me more control over my money, so I worry about it less.

Watching K-dramas and movies used to feel like I was just running away from my tasks. Now I use an episode as a break or a reward, and I come back relaxed and ready to work again. Sleep and good food help my body recover after a long day, and I can't study or train well without them. Writing haikus helps me turn heavy feelings into something small and clear. Talking with my family reminds me that I don't have to carry everything alone. Taking pictures makes me slow down and notice good things, even on a long ride home.

Changing the way I think helps too, because telling myself "one task at a time" makes a busy week feel possible. I also try to see a lost competition as practice and not as proof that I'm not good enough. When I follow this plan, I have more energy, I finish more of my outputs, and I feel calmer. Stress will still come, but now I have a way to recharge. That way, I can keep going after my goals, especially my dream of a career in cybersecurity.
